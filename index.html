01THE STORY

A century of reinvention

One name.
Many eras.

Marlboro began in the 1920s as a cigarette marketed toward women. Its identity changed dramatically in the 1950s, when the brand adopted the red-and-white pack and a more rugged advertising direction.

The resulting visual language was direct and memorable: a sharp chevron, high contrast, and imagery built around independence. This page looks at that language as a piece of design history.

03THE LEGACY

A cultural footprint

Built to
stand apart.

Few consumer brands have maintained such a consistent visual grammar across generations. Marlboro’s packaging, typography, photography, and campaign language became a case study in how repetition can build recognition.

Its history is also inseparable from the questions raised by advertising, public health, and the responsibilities of visual persuasion. Understanding the design means understanding both its power and its context.
